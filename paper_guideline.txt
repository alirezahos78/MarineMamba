Draft:

  Fine-grained visual recognition of marine and aquatic species is critical
  for biodiversity monitoring, ecological research, and fisheries management.
  Deep learning models have achieved high accuracy on controlled benchmarks,
  yet most rely on large-capacity transformers that are ill-suited for
  resource-constrained deployment and suffer from class imbalance common in
  real underwater imagery.

  We propose PyramidCLIPSpyMamba, a lightweight dual-branch state-space model
  that classifies aquatic species from frozen CLIP spatial features extracted
  at two resolutions. Each branch processes a spiral-ordered token sequence
  through bidirectional Mamba blocks augmented with CBAM (channel-and-spatial
  attention), LayerScale, and stochastic depth regularization; the CLIP CLS
  token is injected as a global context anchor at the head of the spatial
  sequence, enabling simultaneous local-to-global reasoning within a single
  linear-complexity pass. The fine-resolution branch (ViT-B/16, 14×14 patches)
  and coarse-resolution branch (ViT-B/32, 7×7 patches) are concatenated and
  passed to an MLP head. The entire trainable component contains only 1.03M
  parameters. Class imbalance is addressed via focal loss with a square-root
  balanced sampler, requiring no generative augmentation.

  We evaluate on three public benchmarks: AQUA20, Sea Animals 23 (Sea23), and
  Fish4Knowledge (Fish4K). Our model achieves 95.2% / 96.78% / 98.46% Top-1
  accuracy and 92% / 96.25% / 88.37% Macro F1, respectively, outperforming
  comparable CNN and transformer baselines while using 10–100× fewer
  trainable parameters.

  SECTION 3 — INTRODUCTION  (~1 page)
================================================================================

Paragraph 1 — Motivation
  Start with the problem: underwater/aquatic biodiversity monitoring requires
  automated species identification. Cite challenges: visual similarity between
  species, complex textures, lighting variations, and severe class imbalance
  (e.g., Fish4K: 12,112 samples for the most common class vs. 11 for the rarest).

Paragraph 2 — Existing approaches and their limitations
  CNN-based models (VGG, ResNet, EfficientNet) are dominant but:
    - require large labeled datasets
    - do not exploit pre-trained visual-language representations
  Transformer-based models (ViT) achieve high accuracy but:
    - are parameter-heavy (hundreds of millions)
    - expensive for deployment on edge devices
  Mamba (state-space models):
    - linear-complexity sequence modeling
    - recent success in vision tasks, but not applied to CLIP feature spaces
    - scanning order matters for 2D spatial features — not addressed before

Paragraph 3 — Our approach in brief
  We freeze CLIP (no fine-tuning), extract spatial patch features at two
  resolutions, and train only the Mamba backbone (1.03M params). Spiral
  scanning provides a natural 2D traversal that is more spatially coherent
  than raster or column-major scans. The CLS token from CLIP is injected
  into each branch as a global semantic anchor. Two branches form a resolution
  pyramid capturing both fine texture (ViT-B/16) and coarse shape (ViT-B/32).

Paragraph 4 — Contributions (use a bulleted list in the paper)
  1. SpyMambaBlock: a Mamba block with bidirectional spiral scanning and
     CBAM attention — the first application of spiral scanning order in
     state-space models for spatial feature sequences.
  2. PyramidCLIPSpyMamba: a dual-resolution architecture that fuses ViT-B/16
     and ViT-B/32 CLIP features via parallel branches with CLS-token injection.
  3. A lightweight (1.03M param) training scheme using focal loss and
     square-root balanced sampling, achieving state-of-the-art results on
     three marine species benchmarks.
  4. Comprehensive evaluation on AQUA20, Sea23, and Fish4K showing
     competitive or superior performance vs. published baselines.

Subsection 4.1 — Marine Species Classification
  Cite papers on:
    - CNN-based fish recognition (ResNet, VGG on Fish4K)
    - Multi-level residual VGGNet [ScienceDirect 2021] achieving 99.69% on Fish4K
      (balanced split) — note the evaluation difference from our imbalanced split
    - AQUA20 baseline CNN works (~92%)
    - Kaggle benchmarks on Sea23 (~88.77%)

Subsection 4.2 — Vision Foundation Models and CLIP
  - CLIP (Radford et al., 2021): contrastive pre-training aligns vision and
    language; spatial patch features encode rich local semantics
  - Frozen CLIP features have been used successfully in action recognition,
    medical imaging — cite 2–3 such papers
  - Emphasize: we do NOT fine-tune CLIP; this keeps the backbone cost-free
    and lets any downstream model benefit from the universal representation

Subsection 4.3 — Mamba and State-Space Models in Vision
  - S4, Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023): linear-complexity sequence modeling
  - VMamba, Vision Mamba, PlainMamba: apply Mamba to image patches
  - Key limitation in prior work: use raster or column-major scanning,
    which breaks 2D spatial locality; spiral scanning is unexplored
  - Our SpyMambaBlock addresses this gap

Subsection 4.4 — Attention Mechanisms
  - CBAM (Woo et al., 2018): channel + spatial attention gates
  - LayerScale (Touvron et al., 2021): learned scalar per token stabilizes
    training of deep architectures
  - Our integration of CBAM into Mamba blocks is novel for this domain

Subsection 4.5 — Class Imbalance in Fine-Grained Recognition
  - Focal loss (Lin et al., 2017): down-weights easy examples
  - Square-root sampling: a softer alternative to uniform reweighting
  - We show this combination outperforms more complex strategies (BBS + SupCon)
    when the architecture itself is already strong

  SECTION 5 — METHOD  (~2.5 pages)
================================================================================

--- 5.1  Feature Extraction (Frozen CLIP)

  We use OpenAI's CLIP with two ViT backbones:
    - ViT-B/16: each image → 14×14 = 196 spatial patch tokens (768-dim each)
                + 1 CLS token (512-dim)
    - ViT-B/32: each image → 7×7 = 49 spatial patch tokens (768-dim each)
                + 1 CLS token (512-dim)

  At training time, 4 augmented views per image are generated:
    [random resized crop, color jitter, horizontal flip, grid-shuffle permutation]
  This block storage layout keeps all views of image j at indices:
    j, j+N, j+2N, j+3N  (N = number of unique images)
  Features are pre-extracted once and stored on disk (eliminates GPU overhead
  for the backbone during training).

  Diagram to include:
    Raw image → [CLIP ViT-B/16] → 14×14 spatial grid + CLS_16
                [CLIP ViT-B/32] → 7×7  spatial grid + CLS_32
                (both frozen; saved as .pt files)

--- 5.2  SpyMambaBlock

  Each block takes an input sequence x ∈ R^{B × L × d}
  where L = 1 + H×W (CLS token prepended to spatial tokens).

  Steps:
    a) LayerNorm → separate CLS token from spatial tokens
    b) SpiralScanner: reorder H×W spatial tokens into two sequences:
         forward spiral  (outside → center)
         backward spiral (center → outside)
       Prepend CLS to each, giving [CLS | spiral-ordered spatial]
    c) Two parallel Mamba passes (shared weights) on forward & backward sequences
    d) Separate CLS outputs from spatial outputs; restore spatial to raster order
    e) Fuse: y_spatial = α * fwd_spatial + (1-α) * bwd_spatial
       where α = sigmoid(learnable scalar, initialized 0.5)
    f) CBAM: reshape y_spatial to [B, d, H, W] → channel attn → spatial attn
    g) Merge CLS back: y = [y_cls | y_spatial_attended]
    h) LayerScale + DropPath residual (two stages: Mamba, FFN)

  Why spiral scanning?
    - Raster order creates a discontinuity at each row end
    - Spiral traversal preserves neighborhood proximity throughout the sequence
    - Mamba's causal dependencies flow through geometrically coherent neighbors

  Include a figure showing: raster order vs. column-major vs. spiral order
  over a 4×4 grid, highlighting which tokens are adjacent in the sequence.

--- 5.3  PyramidBranch

  Input: x (spatial grid [B,C,H,W]) + cls ([B,512])
  Steps:
    1. Flatten and project spatial: Linear(C→d) → [B, H*W, d]
    2. Project CLS: Linear(512→d) → [B, 1, d]; prepend to spatial
    3. Add learned positional embedding (size 1+H*W, dim d)
    4. Pass through N SpyMambaBlocks
    5. LayerNorm → mean-pool all 1+H*W tokens → [B, d]

  Fine branch:  C=768, H=W=14, d=128, N=1  → output [B, 128]
  Coarse branch: C=768, H=W=7,  d=128, N=1  → output [B, 128]

--- 5.4  PyramidCLIPSpyMamba (full model)

  fine_out   = FineBranch(x_B16, cls_B16)    # [B, 128]
  coarse_out = CoarseBranch(x_B32, cls_B32)  # [B, 128]
  z          = concat([fine_out, coarse_out]) # [B, 256]
  logits     = head(z)                        # [B, num_classes]

  Head architecture:
    LayerNorm(256) → Linear(256→512) → GELU → Dropout(0.3) → Linear(512→C)

  Parameter count: 1.031M total
    Fine branch:   0.453M
    Coarse branch: 0.434M
    Head:          0.144M

  Include a full architecture diagram (recommended figure 1):
    [CLIP B/16 spatial] ──→ [Fine SpyMamba Branch] ─┐
    [CLIP B/16 CLS]     ──→                          ├─→ [concat 256d] → [MLP Head] → logits
    [CLIP B/32 spatial] ──→ [Coarse SpyMamba Branch]─┘
    [CLIP B/32 CLS]     ──→

--- 5.5  Training Strategy

  Loss:
    L_total = FocalLoss(γ=2, α=balanced)
    Focal loss formula: FL(p_t) = -(1-p_t)^γ log(p_t)
    α_c = N_total / (C × N_c)   [class-frequency inverse weighting]

  Sampler:
    WeightedRandomSampler with w_c = 1/sqrt(N_c) per class
    This is softer than uniform reweighting; avoids over-sampling very rare classes

  Optimizer: AdamW (lr=3e-4, weight decay=1e-4)
  Scheduler: CosineAnnealingLR + linear warmup (5 epochs)
  Early stopping: patience=15 epochs on validation Top-1
  Batch size: 128 (or 115 for BBS experiments)
  Augmentation: 4 views per image (crop, jitter, flip, grid-shuffle) at extraction time

Main results table (include all three datasets, all metrics):

  | Dataset  | Top-1  | Top-2  | Top-3  | Macro F1 | Params |
  |----------|--------|--------|--------|----------|--------|
  | AQUA20   | 95.2%  |  ~98%  |  ~99%  |  ~92%    | 1.03M  |
  | Sea23    | 96.78% | 99.23% | 99.71% |  96.25%  | 1.03M  |
  | Fish4K   | 98.46% | 99.12% | 99.20% |  88.37%  | 1.03M  |

Discussion points to address:
  1. Top-1 vs Macro F1 gap on Fish4K (98.46% vs 88.37%):
     - Caused by severe imbalance; dominant class fish_01 boosts Top-1
     - fish_08 → fish_14 confusion (38/43 test errors for fish_08) is a
       visual similarity problem, not addressable by sampling alone
     - Macro F1 reflects per-class difficulty fairly; the 88.37% hides that
       most common classes are classified near-perfectly

  2. Sea23 performance (96.78% / 96.25%):
     - High and balanced; Macro F1 ≈ Top-1 confirms the model is unbiased
     - Dataset is near-balanced, so focal loss and sqrt sampler have little work
     - The 8-point gap over the best public CNN is attributable to CLIP features

  3. Efficiency:
     - 1.03M parameters is ~24× fewer than ResNet-50, ~100× fewer than ViT-L
     - Training time: <5 min/epoch (features pre-extracted); practical for research

  4. Scanning order:
     - Spiral order preserves 2D locality in the sequence; CBAM then re-weights
       spatial regions; together they mimic a coarse attention-over-space mechanism

  SECTION 11 — WRITING TIPS

  1. ALWAYS write the method section before the introduction.
     You will know exactly what to motivate once the method is clear.

  2. Do not overstate novelty. Instead say:
     "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first application of
      spiral scanning in Mamba-based vision backbones."

  3. For the Fish4K comparison, explicitly note the split difference:
     "We evaluate on the raw imbalanced split (ratio 1100:1) rather than
      the curated balanced version used in prior work [cite], making our
      evaluation strictly harder."

  4. Put the main results table early (start of Section 6 or as Table 1).
     Reviewers look for results tables first.

  5. Cite the baseline for everything:
     - Mamba: Gu & Dao, "Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with
               Selective State Spaces", 2023
     - CLIP:  Radford et al., "Learning Transferable Visual Models From
               Natural Language Supervision", ICML 2021
     - CBAM:  Woo et al., "CBAM: Convolutional Block Attention Module",
               ECCV 2018
     - Focal: Lin et al., "Focal Loss for Dense Object Detection",
               ICCV 2017
     - LayerScale: Touvron et al., "Going Deeper with Image Transformers",
               ICCV 2021

  6. Abstract should stand alone: someone who only reads the abstract must
     know your method name, datasets, and key numbers.

  7. Use passive voice sparingly — "we propose", "we evaluate" is clearer.

  8. Figures should be vector graphics (PDF or SVG), not screenshots.
     Use matplotlib or draw.io for architecture diagrams.
